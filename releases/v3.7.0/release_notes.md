# Klip v3.7.0

### Added

- **Keep Open: paste several clips without Klip closing each time** - A new window button at the bottom-left of the history window, beside the sidebar and preview toggles (**⌥⌘K**, or Settings → General → Window). With it on, Klip stays on screen after you paste instead of closing behind the clip, so filling in a form or assembling a document from several clips is one trip rather than one trip per clip. It is deliberately not called a pin: a clip's pin floats that clip to the top of the list and belongs to the clip, this belongs to the window. Pasting still hands the keyboard to the app you pasted into, so pick the next clip with the mouse - or press your Klip hotkey, which puts the keyboard back in Klip rather than closing it while the mode is on. Esc still closes the window, and the setting is remembered between launches.
- **Kind of a clip, in the preview pane** - The info list under the preview now says what the thing actually is: "PNG image", "JPEG image", "PDF document", "Word document", "Excel spreadsheet", "Numbers spreadsheet", "ZIP archive", "MP4 video" and so on, above Size - the same line, and the same word, that Finder's Get Info uses. Text clips get it too, where it says "Plain text" or "Rich text", which is the one place Klip tells you whether pasting a clip will carry its formatting. The common formats are named the way people name them rather than the way the system does - macOS calls a `.xlsx` an "Office Open XML spreadsheet" and answers nothing at all for a `.numbers` file unless iWork is installed. Anything not in that list still gets the system's own description, so nothing is ever blank.
- **Choose whether the search box remembers what you typed** - Settings → General → Search. Off by default, which means the search box is empty every single time Klip opens. Before this, a query typed to find one clip came back for the next 90 seconds - long enough to reopen Klip, see a nearly empty list, and wonder where the history went. Turn it on and the query stays until you clear it.
- **Double-click the preview image to Quick Look it** - In the preview pane, double-clicking a clip's image opens the same full-size Quick Look panel that Space opens. The pane shrinks anything taller than 240 points, so this is the quickest way to see a screenshot at its real size without leaving Klip.

### Changed

- **Double-clicking a clip pastes it** - Same as pressing ↩, and now literally the same code, so everything ↩ does comes with it: paste-plain when that is your default, a multi-selection pasted as one, restore rather than paste in the Trash, and the window staying up when Keep Open is on. It used to copy the clip to the clipboard and close instead, which meant the two most obvious ways to use a clip did different things and only one of them put the text where you were typing.
- **The preview's info list reads what → where → when** - Kind, Size, Dimensions, Folder, From, Copied. The two facts that repeat across whole runs of clips, the app a clip came from and the minute it arrived, sit at the bottom now instead of on top of the ones you scan for.
- **The divider lines sit on the panel edges** - Each divider's hairline was drawn down the middle of its 12pt grab strip, which put it 6pt outside the panel it separates. Every panel therefore looked like it had 6pt more padding on its divider side than on its window side: the sidebar's rows are 18pt from both of its edges but read as 18 and 24, and the preview's image is centred in its pane to within half a point but read as pushed to one side. The content was never off - the line marking the boundary was. It is now drawn on the edge it marks, and the grab strip is unchanged.
- **The preview's action icons centre on their own line** - When the icons drop below the heading, the row had a single trailing spacer, so every point by which the pane was wider than its minimum piled up on the left of them. It splits evenly now.
- **The side panels stop shrinking past what they hold** - The sidebar's and the preview's minimum widths were fixed numbers narrower than their own contents, so dragged all the way in, the sidebar truncated "Favorites" to "Favor…" and wrapped "New Folder" onto two lines, and the preview clipped its header and its info values off both edges. Both floors are now measured from what the panel actually has to draw, and they scale with the text-size settings. A width saved under the old floor is corrected the next time the window opens.
- **The shortcut legend stops spilling over the panels** - In a narrow window the row of key hints could paint itself across the sidebar and the preview instead of wrapping onto its second line. Every hint is fixed-size, so the row reported its full width as a *minimum*, which the bar could not be laid out narrower than - and the wrap logic then measured that overflowing width and concluded, reasonably, that one row fitted. It now reports no minimum, so the measurement is the real space available and the wrap works as intended; a hint too wide even on a row of its own ellipsises rather than being cut mid-word.
- **Folders can be dragged into any order** - Press a folder in the sidebar, move it, and an accent line shows the gap it will land in - including above the first folder and below the last. Reordering was supposed to work before and never did: the drag was started from a view sitting *behind* the row, which never receives the press, so nothing happened however long you held the mouse down. It is a plain drag now, in the same layer as the row's own click and double-click.
- **Moving between clips no longer makes the preview blink** - Stepping onto an image used to flash. The root of it: the image, its dimensions and the clip's size were loaded in a task that runs *after* the selection has already been drawn, and that task blanked them before refilling them - so every step drew three times, once with the previous clip's picture still under the new clip's heading, once empty, and once correct. The rows that never flickered were exactly the ones read straight off the clip. All three are read that way now, resolved without waiting and cached, so a step is a single change with nothing in between. Five smaller things went with it: the pane cross-faded itself on every selection because text and image clips draw structurally different previews; a spinner flashed in its own differently-sized box before the picture replaced it; the header's action icons were built per clip type, so they changed count, slid sideways, and could wrap onto a second row for one clip and not the next; Kind and Dimensions appeared only once they had values, resizing the info list a beat behind the image; and the tag strip under it changed height from clip to clip, moving every row above.
- **The preview's action icons are always the same eight, in the same order** - Edit, Copy, Save to disk, Extract text, Pin, Favorite, Lock, Delete. One that does not apply to the selected clip is greyed out rather than removed - Edit on an image, Extract text on anything that is not an image - so the icon you are reaching for is in the same place on every clip, and hovering a greyed one says why it is off. Save to disk is now offered on every kind, matching what its keyboard shortcut has always done.
- **Tags are hidden** - The tag chips on rows, the Tags filter chip, the `#tag` search syntax, the tag editor under the preview, ⌘T and the Clear Tag Filter key are all gone from the window and from Settings. Folders cover the same ground here, and having both meant two parallel ways to file and find the same clip. Nothing was deleted: clips keep the tags they already carry, those tags still protect a clip from the history limit, they still sync between Macs, and search still matches them.
- **Tooltips appear the moment you point at an icon** - They used to wait half a second, copying the system delay. Klip's icons are the only label those buttons have, so the answer to "what is this one?" was arriving after you had already moved on.

### Installing

Download `Klip_Universal.zip` (Apple Silicon + Intel), unzip it, and drag **Klip.app** to your Applications folder.

The build is signed with a local identity and is not notarized, so the first launch trips Gatekeeper: right-click Klip.app and choose **Open**, then confirm. After that it launches normally.
